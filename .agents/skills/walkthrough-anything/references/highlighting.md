# What earns a highlight

A highlight is a spotlight plus a card. It says to the viewer: look here, this matters, here is why. Every one costs attention. A video with twenty highlights has none.

## The highlight test

A beat earns a highlight only if you can answer all four with evidence from discovery:

1. **Decision.** What does the viewer decide or do here? If the element supports no decision or action, it is scenery.
2. **Consequence.** What happens because of it? Name the state change or the output. Cite the handler.
3. **Visibility.** Can the recording show the consequence within a few seconds? If the result is invisible, off-screen, or needs a real backend, the beat is a caption at most, not a highlight.
4. **Novelty.** Would a first-time viewer not have guessed it? A labelled "Save" button needs no card. The rule that unlocks the button might.

Write the four answers into the storyboard for every beat. A beat with a blank answer is cut or demoted.

## Always highlight

- The first action the viewer must take to start the job.
- The control that triggers the primary action, and the moment it becomes available.
- The result view, led by the single most important element in it.
- Any rule the interface enforces that a viewer would otherwise trip over (a disabled state with a reason, a prerequisite, a limit).
- Where things are saved or where to find them again.

## Never highlight

- Decoration, branding, layout.
- Anything the viewer cannot act on.
- Two things at once. One spotlight, one card, one idea.
- Elements mid-transition. Wait for the page to settle.
- Settings, preferences and admin before the job is shown. They go last, and only if the viewer needs them to get started.
- Anything that requires a number you did not measure to explain.

## Demote instead of cut

Some things matter but do not need a spotlight:

- **Chapter caption**: a two-word label for where the viewer is. Costs nothing, orients everyone.
- **A breather on the plain page**: letting the viewer look at a finished screen with no overlay is a highlight of the whole.
- **Cursor only**: moving to and clicking something without a card, when the label already explains it.

## Ordering within a chapter

Lead with the decision, follow with the consequence, close with where it went. Never explain the mechanism before the viewer has seen the outcome.

## A worked example

RenewalDesk's result screen has a verdict banner, two policy cards, a list of material changes, a collapsible full comparison and an interactions list.

- Verdict banner: decision (read it), consequence (know whether to bind), visible, novel (the app leads with a judgement, not data). Highlight.
- Policy cards: no decision. Scenery. The breather after the verdict shows them for free.
- Material changes: decision (which to negotiate), consequence (negotiation points later), visible, novel (filtering of noise is the product's value). Highlight.
- Full comparison: decision (dig in or not), visible, novel only in that it is collapsed by design. Highlight briefly, open it once, no second card.
- Interactions list: matters to an expert, not to a first-time viewer. Cut from the video, named in the "left out" note.
